\begin{abstract}
Branching initiation from the corner of a V-shaped interfacial shear crack
lying along a kinked interface between two isotropic elastic materials is
studied under plane strain. The crack is treated as a prescribed stationary
defect with two symmetric branches in frictionless contact. Its corner singular
field serves as the outer field for a small tensile process zone along a
material-sector bisector. Conditions are obtained for simultaneous compressive
contact of the interfacial crack faces and a tensile normal field in the
material containing the zone. Mellin transformation, asymptotic matching, the
Wiener--Hopf method, and stress regularity at the zone tip yield closed-form
relations for the equilibrium zone length, opening, work of separation, and a
zone-related energy parameter. The effects of interface angle, elastic contrast,
and loading are examined. The process zone weakens, but does not remove, the
corner singularity. For fixed geometry, the deformation and energy
characteristics are uniquely related, so consistently calibrated criteria
identify the same branching-initiation state. The formulation also distinguishes
corner branching from competing interfacial propagation at the crack-branch
tips and provides the critical applied load once the relevant material threshold
is specified.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:}
piecewise homogeneous body; kinked interface; V-shaped interfacial shear
crack; process zone; branching initiation; Wiener--Hopf method.
