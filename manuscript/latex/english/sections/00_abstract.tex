\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Purpose:} To develop an analytical model for branching initiation from
the corner of a stationary V-shaped interfacial shear crack at a kinked
bimaterial interface and to determine physically admissible process-zone
states.

\textbf{Methods:} Under plane strain, the crack-corner singular field is used
as the outer field for a small tensile process zone along a material-sector
bisector. Mellin transformation, asymptotic matching, Wiener--Hopf
factorization, and stress regularity at the zone tip are combined to obtain
closed-form relations for the equilibrium process-zone characteristics.

\textbf{Results:} The analysis identifies the admissible material and loading
branches and gives the equilibrium zone length, opening, work of separation,
and zone-related energy parameter. The process zone weakens, but does not
eliminate, the corner singularity. For fixed geometry, the deformation and
energy characteristics are uniquely related. Corner branching and
interfacial propagation from the crack-branch tips emerge as competing local
mechanisms.

\textbf{Conclusion:} The formulation links the singular field of a stationary
V-shaped interfacial shear crack to a closed analytical branching-initiation
criterion and determines the critical applied load once the relevant material
threshold is specified.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:}
piecewise homogeneous body; kinked interface; V-shaped interfacial shear
crack; process zone; branching initiation; Wiener--Hopf method.
