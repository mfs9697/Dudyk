\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Purpose:} To develop an analytical model for branching initiation from
the corner of a stationary V-shaped interfacial shear crack lying along a
kinked interface between two isotropic elastic materials.

\textbf{Methods:} The crack is treated as a prescribed defect with two
symmetric branches in frictionless contact. Its corner singular field is
matched to a small tensile process zone along a material-sector bisector.
Mellin transformation, Wiener--Hopf factorization, asymptotic matching, and
stress regularity at the zone tip are used to determine the local equilibrium
state.

\textbf{Results:} Closed-form relations are obtained for the equilibrium zone
length, opening, work of separation, and a zone-related energy parameter.
Physical admissibility is established from simultaneous compressive crack-face
contact and tensile normal stress in the material containing the zone. The
effects of interface angle, elastic contrast, and loading are quantified. The
process zone weakens, but does not eliminate, the corner singularity.

\textbf{Conclusion:} For fixed geometry, consistently calibrated deformation
and energy criteria identify the same branching-initiation state. The model
also distinguishes corner branching from competing interfacial propagation at
the crack-branch tips and determines the critical applied load once the
relevant material threshold is specified.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:}
piecewise homogeneous body; kinked interface; V-shaped interfacial shear
crack; process zone; branching initiation; Wiener--Hopf method.
