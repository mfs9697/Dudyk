\begin{abstract}
Branching initiation from the corner of a V-shaped interfacial shear crack
lying along a kinked interface between two isotropic elastic materials is
investigated under plane strain. The crack is treated as a prescribed stationary
defect with two symmetric branches whose faces are in frictionless contact. The
singular field at its corner is used as the outer field for a small tensile
process zone oriented along the bisector of one of the material sectors.
Conditions are established for the simultaneous occurrence of compressive
contact between the interfacial crack faces and a tensile normal field in the
material containing the zone. Mellin transformation, asymptotic
matching, the Wiener--Hopf method, and the stress-regularity condition at the
zone tip yield closed analytical relations for the equilibrium zone length,
its opening, the work done against resistance to separation, and the associated
zone-related energy parameter. Their dependence on the interface angle, elastic contrast, and loading level
is examined. The process-zone configuration is shown to have a weaker singularity than
the corresponding configuration with the V-shaped interfacial shear crack
alone, although the singularity is not eliminated completely. For fixed geometry, the energy and deformation
characteristics of the zone are uniquely related; the corresponding
branching-initiation criteria therefore select the same critical state when
their critical parameters are calibrated consistently. Once the corresponding critical characteristic is specified independently,
the results allow the critical external load for branching initiation to be
determined.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:}
piecewise homogeneous body; kinked interface; V-shaped interfacial shear
crack; process zone; branching initiation; Wiener--Hopf method.
