\begin{abstract}
Under plane strain, branching initiation from a V-shaped interfacial shear
crack at the corner of a V-shaped interface between two isotropic elastic
materials is investigated. The crack is treated as a prescribed stationary
defect with symmetric branches whose faces are in frictionless contact. The
singular field at its corner is used as the outer field for a small tensile
process zone oriented along the bisector of one of the material sectors.
Conditions are established for the simultaneous occurrence of compressive
contact between the interfacial-crack faces and a tensile normal field in the
material containing the zone. Mellin transformation, asymptotic
matching, the Wiener--Hopf method, and the zone-tip stress-regularity
condition yield closed analytical relations for the equilibrium zone length,
its opening, the work done against separation resistance, and the associated
zone-related energy parameter. Their dependence on the interface angle, elastic contrast, and loading level
is examined. The
process-zone configuration is shown to have a weaker singularity than the
underlying V-shaped interfacial-crack configuration, although the singularity
is not eliminated completely. For fixed geometry, the energy and deformation
characteristics of the zone are uniquely related; the corresponding
branching-initiation criteria are therefore equivalent when their critical
material parameters are chosen consistently. The results provide a basis for predicting branching initiation once the
relevant material fracture-resistance parameter is specified independently.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:}
piecewise homogeneous body; kinked interface; V-shaped interfacial shear
crack; process zone; branching initiation; Wiener--Hopf method.
