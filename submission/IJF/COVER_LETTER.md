On behalf of my co-authors, I am pleased to submit the manuscript entitled **“Analytical Model for Branching Initiation from the Corner of a V-Shaped Interfacial Shear Crack”** for consideration as a Research Article in the *International Journal of Fracture*.

The paper develops an analytical fracture-mechanics model for branching initiation from the corner of a prescribed stationary V-shaped interfacial shear crack at a kinked bimaterial interface. The contacting crack generates a power-law singular corner field, which is used as the outer field for a small tensile process zone in one of the constituent materials. By combining Mellin transformation, asymptotic matching, and the Wiener–Hopf method, we obtain closed relations for the equilibrium process-zone length, opening, work of separation, and a zone-related energy parameter. We also establish the simultaneous physical-admissibility conditions associated with compressive contact of the interfacial crack faces and tensile loading of the prospective branching zone, and examine the effects of interface angle, elastic contrast, and loading.

The manuscript is closely connected to, but distinct from, two earlier lines of work. The recent *International Journal of Fracture* paper by Kaminsky, Dudyk, and Chornoivan (2026, DOI 10.1007/s10704-026-00916-z) concerns the influence of T-stress on a process zone associated with kinking from the **tip of a conventional interfacial crack**. The present problem is geometrically and asymptotically different: the starting defect is a **stationary V-shaped interfacial shear crack with two contacting branches**, and branching is studied from its **corner point**, where the singular field has an angle- and elastic-contrast-dependent exponent. Conversely, the works of Nazarenko and Kipnis on V-shaped interfacial shear cracks determine the elastic corner field and the limiting equilibrium of the crack branches. The present study uses that stationary cracked field as the outer asymptotics and introduces a tensile process zone at the corner to formulate a local branching-initiation problem. Thus, it addresses a fracture mechanism not treated in those elastic crack-equilibrium studies.

A further point of the present work is the explicit separation of two competing local mechanisms in the same stationary configuration: branching from the corner and interfacial propagation from the crack-branch tips. The analysis also clarifies the relation between deformation and zone-related energy criteria and the range in which the local small-scale approximation can be interpreted quantitatively.

We believe that the manuscript fits the scope of the *International Journal of Fracture* because it provides a theoretical contribution to interface fracture mechanics, singular fields, process-zone modelling, and fracture initiation.

The manuscript has not been published previously and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the manuscript and its submission to the *International Journal of Fracture*.
